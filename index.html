Fall risk, early

We watch how walking changes week to week.

In the highest-risk homes, a small radar on the path to the bathroom tracks walking speed each week, and door sensors count night-time trips. When the same person slows down week after week, Abri flags it before a fall.

Private by design

The kit has no camera and no microphone.

Only the aide records, on their own phone, after she leaves the room. The kit goes only to the homes that need it most, often funded by the agency's insurer, and it sends only small events like “walk took 8.6 seconds.”

Questions

Do we need hardware to start?

No. Debriefs, risk scores, coaching and the evidence record work on aides' phones alone. The radar kit is added only in the highest-risk homes.

Is this a medical device?

No. Abri tracks safety and activity trends and suggests practical steps like a PT home-safety visit. It doesn't diagnose or replace clinical judgment.

Does it replace EVV?

No. Abri's presence record complements your state's electronic visit verification. It isn't a substitute for it.

Is Abri an insurance company?

No. Abri is software for home care agencies. Your coverage stays with your insurer. Insurers and brokers who partner with Abri use its safety reports to reward agencies that run safe operations, and can fund safety kits for high-risk homes.
